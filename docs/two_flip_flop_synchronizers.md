# TWO FLIP-FLOP SYNCHRONIZERS

This note explains how Two Flip-Flop Synchronizers work and how they help in **Clock Domain Crossing (CDC)**.

---

## Why Synchronizers

We use synchronizers to understand how we can send data from one clock domain to another, in this experiment, we send data from one clock domain of **25MHz** to another of **100MHz** and we use the Two-flop synchronization method to do so.

## About

In this experiment, we take two clock domains A and B, we transmit data from a slower domain to a faster domain, the signal *async_signal* is controlled by clock A and is the *wire* that transfers data from domain A to B.

## The CDC Problem

A signal generated by one clock domain can change at any arbitrary moment with reference to another.

If the receiving flip-flop samples the signal close to its clock edge, its setup or hold time can be violated.

This can cause the flip-flop to enter a metastable state.

*Metastability* cannot be completely eliminated. Instead, the design must reduce the probability that metastability propagates into the rest of the receiving logic.

## Two-Flip-Flop Synchronizer

The solution for a single-bit signal is to pass it through two flip-flops clocked by the destination clock.

The idea is that one flip-flop might enter a metastable state as it samples the signal on the clock edge, but it then gets another complete clock cycle to settle down before the next flip-flop samples it, which diminishes the possibility of a metastable output by a large factor.

In this experiment, the clock A sends the data at its rising edge and the flip-flop *sync_1* samples at the first positive clock edge of B, then after another rising edge of B the flip-flop *sync_2* samples it again, this gives the *sync_1* flip-flop enough time to stabilize.


```verilog
always @(posedge clk_B) begin
    if (rst) begin
        sync_1 <= 0;
        sync_2 <= 0;
    end
    else begin
        sync_1 <= async_signal;
        sync_2 <= sync_1;
    end
end
```
